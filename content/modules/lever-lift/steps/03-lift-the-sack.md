## Do: build the lever (10 minutes)

1. **Check the plank** for cracks, splits and big knots. Show the guide.
2. Tape the pipe on top of the brick, crossways. Put the brick on a flat, hard floor.
3. Find the plank's **balance point**: lay the empty plank across the pipe and slide it until it just balances. Mark that point. This is your pivot. With the pivot here, the plank's own weight doesn't turn it either way.
4. From the pivot, mark the plank every 10 cm on both sides. On one side, mark the point **1.00 m** from the pivot and tie a rope loop there. That's where you pull.
5. Weigh the sack by hanging it from the luggage scale (two people, bent knees). Write down its mass.

## Theory: weight

Read the section *Weight* in **Forces and motion**. The sack's weight, in newtons, is its mass × 9.8. The luggage scale reads in kilograms. That's fine: on a lever, every force is multiplied by the same 9.8, so you can do the whole calculation in kilograms and still get the right answer. Then multiply by 9.8 at the end if you want newtons.

## Do: measure (15 minutes)

1. Hook the luggage scale onto the rope loop.
2. Pull **straight down** on the scale, slowly, until the sack's end of the plank just lifts off the floor. Read the scale at that moment. Then lower it slowly.
3. Do it three times for each distance and take the mean.

**No luggage scale?** Hang a strong bag from the rope loop and add full 1 L water bottles (about 1 kg each) and then 0.5 L bottles until the sack just lifts. Count the mass in the bag.

## Notice something puzzling

Your measured pulls are probably a little **bigger** than your predictions. Why? Think about the pipe rubbing on the plank, and whether you pulled exactly straight down. Is your difference less than 20%?

## Do it better: what does it cost? (10 minutes)

Put the sack back at 0.30 m. One person holds a tape measure upright next to the rope loop, another next to the sack.

1. Pull the loop down slowly by 10 cm. How far does the sack go up?
2. Work out: pull × distance your hand moved, and sack's weight × distance the sack rose.

## Record

Fill in the table and the percentage difference for each row. Write one sentence: "The lever let me lift ___ kg with a pull of only ___ kg, but my hand had to move ___ times as far as the sack."
