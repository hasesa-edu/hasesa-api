## The plank rules first

Before anything is lifted, your guide reads out the plank rules. Copy them into your notebook:

1. Check the plank for cracks before every lift.
2. Feet and hands away from under the plank, the sack and the plank ends.
3. Lift slowly, lower slowly. Never let go suddenly.
4. Nobody stands, sits or jumps on the plank.
5. Never more than 15 kg of sand.

## Watch

Your guide puts a 15 kg sack of sand on the floor. Who thinks they can lift it with **two fingers**? A volunteer tries, hooking two fingers into the knot. (Don't strain: stop if it doesn't move.)

Now the guide lays a long plank across a brick, close to its middle, and ties the sack onto the plank a short way from the brick. A loop of rope hangs from the far end. The smallest person in the room hooks two fingers into the loop and pulls down gently.

Look carefully at:

- how far the person's hand moves, and how far the sack rises,
- what happens when the guide moves the sack **further from** the brick,
- what happens when the person pulls **closer to** the brick.

## Predict

On a page titled *The lift*, write:

1. **Where did the extra force come from?** Write your best guess.
2. Was the pull on the rope **more or less** than the sack's weight? How many times less, do you think?
3. **Guess:** what would happen if the sack sat right on top of the brick?

## Questions

As a team, list every question you have. For example: Does a longer plank always help? Is there a rule that tells you exactly how hard to pull? Why do builders use long iron bars? Is the lever giving us free energy?
