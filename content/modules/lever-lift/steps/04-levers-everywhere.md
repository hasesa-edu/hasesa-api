## Theory: three classes of lever

Read the last two sections of **Levers and moments**: *Mechanical advantage, and what it costs* and *Three classes of lever*. The class depends on what is in the middle: the pivot, the load or the effort.

## Do: sort the levers (10 minutes)

Your guide puts out scissors, a bottle opener, a hammer and a nail in a block of wood, and a picture of a wheelbarrow. For each one, and for your own **forearm** lifting a water bottle:

1. Draw it and label the **pivot**, the **load** and the **effort**.
2. Say which class it is.
3. Say whether it makes your force **bigger** (effort further from the pivot than the load) or makes the load move **further and faster**.

Then find one more lever in the room, or one you use at home.

## Back to the mystery (5 minutes)

Open your page titled *The lift*. Next to each guess, write ✓ or ✗ and what you now know.

Answer the question properly, in your own words: **where did the extra force come from?** Use these words: *pivot, moment, distance, law of the lever, further*.

## Explain it to the guide (10 minutes)

Your guide will ask each of you about your lever. Be ready to:

- show your ruler table and say the law of the lever,
- show how you predicted one of the pulls, with the working,
- say how close your measurement was, and why it wasn't exact,
- explain what the lever "costs" you, using your hand and sack distances,
- name the class of each lever you sorted, and say why your forearm is built for speed, not strength.
