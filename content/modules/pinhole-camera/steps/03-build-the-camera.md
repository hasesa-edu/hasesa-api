## Do: build the tubes (20 minutes)

You make two tubes. One slides inside the other, so you can change the camera's length.

**Inner tube (the screen).**

1. Roll an A3 sheet of card into a tube 42 cm long and about 8 cm across. The edges overlap; that's fine. Tape it.
2. Cut a circle of tracing paper a little bigger than the tube's end. With a ruler and a sharp pencil, draw a line across the middle and mark every 0.5 cm along it. This is your **scale**.
3. Tape the tracing paper tightly over one end, like a drum. This end is the **screen**.

**Outer tube (the pinhole).**

1. Roll the second sheet round the inner tube, loosely enough that the inner tube slides, and tape it.
2. Cut a card disc to cover one end. Cut a 2 cm square window in the middle of it and tape it on.
3. Tape a 4 cm square of foil over the window. Push a pin straight through the middle of the foil to make a neat, round hole.

Slide the inner tube into the outer one, **screen end first**, so the screen is inside the camera facing the pinhole. You look into the open end of the inner tube.

**Mark the distances.** Measure the length of the outer tube, $L$. On the outside of the inner tube, measuring from the screen end, draw a line at $L - 15$ cm and another at $L - 30$ cm. When a line sits exactly at the back edge of the outer tube, the screen is 15 cm or 30 cm from the pinhole.

## Do: first picture (5 minutes)

Stand in the shade and point the pinhole at a bright scene: a sunlit wall, a tree, the school building. Never at the Sun. Put the cloth over your head and the back of the camera, so no light gets in except through the pinhole. Look at the screen. Slide the inner tube in and out.

## Do: hole size (10 minutes)

Keep the camera length the same and look at the same scene each time. Try three foil squares:

- a **pin** hole,
- a **nail** hole (about 3 mm),
- a **pencil** hole (about 7 mm).

For each, give the image a score from 1 to 5 for **brightness** and from 1 to 5 for **sharpness**. Each person scores alone, then compare.

## Do: camera length (7 minutes)

Use the pin hole. Pick an object with a clear top and bottom, like a door or a window across the yard. Set the screen to 15 cm from the pinhole, and use the scale to measure the image height. Then set it to 30 cm and measure again. Before you measure, predict: what will happen to the image height?

## Record (3 minutes)

Make two tables: hole size with brightness and sharpness scores, and screen distance with image height. Answer:

- Which hole gave the best picture? Why can't you have the brightest and the sharpest at the same time?
- When the screen distance doubled, what happened to the image height? Does that match $\dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = \dfrac{d_i}{d_o}$?

Put the pin-hole foil back on before the next step.
