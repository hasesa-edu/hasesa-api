## Theory: rearranging formulas

Your camera gives you $h_i$ and $d_i$, and a tape measure gives you $d_o$. You want $h_o$. Read **Rearranging formulas** to learn the golden rule: do the same thing to both sides.

Start from

$$\frac{h_i}{h_o} = \frac{d_i}{d_o}$$

Flip both sides over (still true), then multiply both sides by $h_i$:

$$h_o = \frac{h_i \times d_o}{d_i}$$

## Do: measure something tall (10 minutes)

1. Choose a tree, flagpole or building on the compound. Everyone writes down a guess of its height.
2. Set the screen to 30 cm. Stand far enough back that the **whole** object fits on your screen, with room to spare. (If it won't fit, step back or use the 15 cm mark.) Measure your distance to the base, $d_o$, with the tape measure, in metres.
3. Under the hood, line up the image so its bottom sits on the end of your scale. Read where the top reaches. That's $h_i$. Two people read it, and take the mean.
4. Write down $d_i$, the distance from the pinhole to the screen (30 cm or 15 cm, from your marks).
5. Change $h_i$ and $d_i$ into metres, then calculate $h_o$.

**Worked example.** A tree's image is 5.4 cm tall on a screen 30 cm behind the pinhole, and you are standing 20 m away.

$$h_o = \frac{0.054 \times 20}{0.30} = 3.6 \text{ m}$$

## Do it better: check it a second way (4 minutes)

Ask a friend whose height you know to stand right next to the object. Measure their image too, $h_f$. Because they're the same distance away,

$$h_o = \text{friend's height} \times \frac{h_i}{h_f}$$

Do your two answers agree within about 10%? If not, which measurement do you trust least?

## Back to the mystery

Open the page titled *The picture from a hole* from step 1. Next to each guess, write ✓ or ✗ and what you now know. Then answer properly, in your own words: **where does the picture come from, and why is it upside down?** Use these words: *straight lines, rays, hole, cross, top, bottom*.

## Explain it to the guide (7 minutes)

Your guide will ask each of you about your camera. Be ready to:

- show a clear image on your screen,
- draw a ray diagram and explain why the image is upside down,
- explain why a bigger hole makes a brighter but blurrier picture,
- show your height measurement, with the rearranged formula and every unit,
- say how your eye and a phone camera get a picture that is both bright **and** sharp. (Hint: the last section of *Light and images*.)
