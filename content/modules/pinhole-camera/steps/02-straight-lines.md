## Do: three cards and a torch (10 minutes)

1. Make a hole about 5 mm across in the middle of three index cards, at exactly the same height. Stack the cards and push a pencil through all three at once so the holes match.
2. Stand the cards up in a row, about 15 cm apart, with modelling clay or folded tape.
3. Shine a torch through the first hole. Look from the other end, or hold a sheet of white paper behind the last card.
4. Now slide the middle card 1 cm to the side. What happens?
5. Thread a string through all three holes and pull it tight. What do you notice about the line of holes when the light gets through?

## Do: the upside-down F (10 minutes)

Work in the darkest corner of the room.

1. Tape a cut-out letter **F** over the front of a torch, so the light comes out in the shape of an F.
2. Make a pinhole in the middle of a card with a pin.
3. Hold the torch about 20 cm in front of the card, and a sheet of white paper about 20 cm behind it.
4. Look at the paper. Which way up is the F? Which way do its arms point?
5. Move the paper back to 40 cm. What happens to the size of the F, and its brightness?

## Notice something puzzling

The F on the paper is upside down. The pinhole has no glass in it, nothing to bend or flip the light. So how did the picture get turned over? And why did it get bigger as the paper moved away?

## Theory: light and images

Read **Light and images**. You'll learn:

- that light travels in straight lines called rays,
- how a pinhole lets just one narrow bundle of rays through from each point of an object,
- why the rays cross at the hole, so the image is upside down,
- how similar triangles give $\dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = \dfrac{d_i}{d_o}$,
- how hole size and camera length change brightness and sharpness.

## Do it better: ray diagrams (12 minutes)

Draw a ray diagram of your F experiment to scale:

1. Draw heights full size, but shrink the distances: 5 cm on the page for every 20 cm in real life. (Rays stay straight when you do this, so the image height still comes out right.) Draw a vertical line 3 cm tall for the F (the object), a dot 5 cm to its right for the hole, and a vertical line 5 cm beyond the hole for the paper.
2. Draw a straight ray from the **top** of the F through the hole to the paper. Then one from the **bottom**.
3. Measure the image on your drawing. How tall is it? Which way up?
4. Draw it again with the paper at 40 cm (10 cm on the page).

## Record

Under your diagrams, write two sentences: why the image is upside down, and why it gets bigger when the screen moves further from the hole.
