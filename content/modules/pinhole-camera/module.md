A tiny hole in a dark room paints a moving, upside-down picture of the world outside. In two hours you find out why, build a camera with no lens, and use it to measure the height of something you can't climb.

1. **A picture from a hole** (15 min). Sit in the guide's dark room and watch the street appear on the wall.
2. **Light in straight lines** (35 min). Line up cards with holes, make an upside-down F, and draw ray diagrams.
3. **Build the camera** (45 min). A sliding-tube camera with a pinhole and a screen. Test hole size and camera length.
4. **How tall is it?** (25 min). Use your camera and similar triangles to measure a tree, flagpole or building.

By the end you'll understand why light makes shadows and pictures, how similar triangles connect the size of an image to the real thing, and why your eye and your phone use a lens instead of a pinhole.
