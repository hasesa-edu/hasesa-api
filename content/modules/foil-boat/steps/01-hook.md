## Watch

Your guide has a bowl of water, a steel bolt and two sheets of foil the same size.

1. The bolt goes in. Where does it end up?
2. One sheet of foil is squeezed into a tight ball and goes in. What happens?
3. The other sheet has been folded into a little boat. It goes in, and then the guide adds coins, one at a time.

Count the coins out loud. When does the boat start to sink?

## Notice something puzzling

The ball and the boat are made of exactly the same foil. They have exactly the same mass. One sinks, one floats.

And the same is true of real ships. A ship that brings fuel, wheat or cars into Djibouti port for Ethiopia is made of steel, and it carries thousands of tonnes on top. A steel bolt the size of your finger can't even keep itself up.

## Predict

On a page titled *The boat mystery*, write:

1. **Why does the ball sink and the boat float?** Your best guess, then a second guess.
2. **What is the water doing to the boat?** Draw the boat and add arrows for any pushes or pulls you think are acting on it.
3. **Guess:** if you had a 20 cm × 20 cm sheet of foil, how many coins could your best boat carry? Each person writes a number. You'll find out in step 3.

## Questions

As a team, list your questions. For example:

- Does a bigger boat always carry more?
- Why does the boat sit lower in the water as coins go in?
- Would it carry more in salty sea water?

Here is your first clue: it has something to do with **how much space** the foil takes up, not just how heavy it is.
