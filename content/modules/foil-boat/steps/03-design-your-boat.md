## The challenge

Every team gets the same: one 20 cm × 20 cm sheet of foil (you may have two spares for practice). No tape, no other materials. The boat that carries the most coins before it sinks wins.

Before you fold, weigh **10 coins** together and divide by 10 to get the mass of one coin. That is more accurate than weighing one coin.

## Theory: why the water pushes up

Read **Pressure**. You'll learn:

- that pressure is force spread over an area, $P = F/A$,
- that 1 cm² = 0.0001 m²,
- how the particles of a gas push on every surface they bump into. Water's particles push in the same way.

One extra fact you need: water presses harder the **deeper** you go. You can feel it in your ears at the bottom of a swimming pool. At a depth $h$, the extra pressure from the water is

$$P = \rho g h$$

where $\rho = 1000$ kg/m³ for water and $g = 9.8$ N/kg.

The water pushes on the bottom of your boat, upwards. The deeper the bottom sits, the harder it pushes. For a boat with a 14 cm × 14 cm base (0.0196 m²) sitting 2 cm (0.02 m) deep:

$$P = 1000 \times 9.8 \times 0.02 = 196\ \text{Pa}$$

$$F = P \times A = 196 \times 0.0196 \approx 3.8\ \text{N}$$

That's enough to hold up about 390 g. Check: the boat is pushing aside $14 \times 14 \times 2 = 392$ cm³ of water, which has a mass of 392 g. Pressure and Archimedes give the same answer.

## Design (15 minutes)

Think about the shape before you fold:

- A wide, flat base means each centimetre the boat sinks pushes aside **lots** of water.
- Tall walls let it sink deeper before water pours in. But every centimetre of wall uses up foil from the base.
- If the sheet is 20 cm wide and the walls are $h$ cm high, the base is $20 - 2h$ cm wide.

## Predict (5 minutes)

Measure your finished boat with a ruler: inside length, inside width, wall height. Weigh it.

$$\text{volume (cm}^3) = \text{length} \times \text{width} \times \text{height}$$

$$\text{maximum load (g)} = \text{volume} \times 1\ \text{g/cm}^3 - \text{boat mass}$$

$$\text{maximum coins} = \text{maximum load} \div \text{mass of one coin}$$

Write your prediction down **before** you test.

## Do: test it (20 minutes)

Float the boat in the basin. Add coins **one at a time**, spreading them evenly over the base. Count out loud. The moment water comes over the side, stop: the last number before it sank is your load.

If you have time, use a spare sheet to make a better boat. Change **one** thing, for example the wall height, and test again.

## Record

Draw your boat with its measurements. Write your prediction, your result, and the difference. Give one reason why your boat carried less (or more) than you predicted.
