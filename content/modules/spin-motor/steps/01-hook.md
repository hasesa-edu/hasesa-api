## The rules first

Before anything spins, your guide reads out the rules. Copy them into your notebook:

1. One 1.5 V cell only. Never mains, never a car battery.
2. Run the motor for 10 seconds at most, then lift the coil off. The wire gets hot.
3. Keep neodymium magnets apart and away from phones and bank cards.
4. Magnets never go near your mouth.
5. Sandpaper for scraping. Only the guide uses a blade.

## Watch

Your guide has a battery lying on its side, two paper clips held to its ends with a rubber band, and a small loop of copper wire resting in the paper clips. A magnet sits on the battery, just under the loop.

The guide gives the loop a gentle flick. Look carefully at:

- how fast it turns, and whether it keeps going,
- what happens when the guide lifts one end of the loop off its paper clip,
- what happens when the guide takes the magnet away,
- what happens when the guide turns the magnet over.

## Predict

On a page titled *The spinning coil*, write:

1. **What makes the loop turn?** Write your best guess, then a second one.
2. **Why does it stop when the magnet is taken away?**
3. **Which way will it spin if the battery is turned round?** Write your guess. You'll test it in step 3.

## Questions

As a team, list every question you have. For example:

- Is the copper wire magnetic?
- Why doesn't the loop just stop at one position, like a compass needle?
- Why does the guide only run it for a few seconds?
- How do real motors, like the one in a fan, do the same thing?

Here is a clue: **an electric current and a magnet can push on each other.** In the next step you'll see the first half of that.
