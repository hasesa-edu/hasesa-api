## Do: the fastest motor in the world (5 minutes)

Stick a neodymium disc magnet to the flat (−) end of an AA or D cell. Stand the cell on the magnet.

Bend 15 cm of bare copper wire into a shape that rests on the top (+) bump of the battery, with both ends hanging down to lightly touch the sides of the magnet. Let go.

The wire spins. Hold it for no more than 10 seconds: it gets hot. Turn the magnet over and try again. Which way does it spin now?

## Do: a compass and a wire (8 minutes)

1. Put a compass flat on the table and let the needle settle pointing north.
2. Lay a stripped piece of insulated wire straight across the top of the compass, lined up **along** the needle.
3. Touch the two bare ends to the ends of a D cell for 2–3 seconds only. Watch the needle.
4. Swap the ends over (reverse the current) and try again.
5. Repeat with the wire 5 cm above the compass.

Record: which way did the needle swing each time, and how far?

## Notice something puzzling

Copper isn't magnetic. A paper clip won't stick to a copper wire. So why does the compass needle move when, and only when, a current flows? And why does reversing the battery reverse the swing?

## Theory: electromagnetism

Read the first two parts of **Electromagnetism**: "A current makes a magnetic field" and "A magnet pushes on a current". You'll learn:

- that every current makes a magnetic field in circles round the wire,
- that a coil of wire acts like a bar magnet, and an iron core makes it much stronger,
- that a wire carrying a current in a magnetic field feels a force, $F = BIL$,
- that reversing the current reverses the field and the force.

Then read the "Current" section of **Charge, current and voltage**, so you know what an amp is. A bare wire straight across a battery lets a very big current flow, a few amps, which is why the wire gets warm.

## Do it better: a nail electromagnet (12 minutes)

1. Wind 20 turns of insulated wire tightly round an iron nail, leaving 20 cm free at each end.
2. Touch the ends to the D cell and dip the nail tip into a pile of small paper clips. Count how many it lifts. Disconnect.
3. Wind 20 more turns on top, in the **same direction**, to make 40. Count again.
4. Try it with no nail inside the coil (slide the coil off). Count again.

Keep each test under 10 seconds. Repeat each one twice and use the bigger count.

## Record

Make a table: number of turns, nail or no nail, paper clips lifted. Then write one sentence for each:

- What does a current do to the space around it?
- What made the electromagnet stronger?
- The wire got warm. Where did that energy come from?
