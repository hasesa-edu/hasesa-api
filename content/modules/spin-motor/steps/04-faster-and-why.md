## Do: the speed challenge (8 minutes)

Change **one thing at a time** and compare with your first motor. Listen to the buzz and watch the blur: faster motors hum higher.

- a stronger magnet (stack two or three neodymium discs, or use a bigger ceramic magnet),
- the coil closer to the magnet (but not touching),
- a new coil with more turns (25 instead of 15), if there's time.

Record each change and whether the motor got faster, slower or stayed the same. Keep every run under 10 seconds.

## Do: the numbers (5 minutes)

Your coil has 15 turns. Only the bottom of each turn is close to the magnet, about 1.5 cm of wire per turn, so $L \approx 15 \times 0.015 = 0.225$ m. Near the coil, the magnet's field is about $B = 0.05$ T, and the current is about $I = 2$ A.

1. Use $F = BIL$ to estimate the push on the coil.
2. The coil weighs about 2 g. Work out its weight, $W = mg$, with $g = 9.8$ N/kg. Compare it with the push.
3. Use $Q = It$ to find how much charge flows in a 10-second run. Then use $E = QV$ to find how much energy the 1.5 V cell gives out. Most of it heats the wire and the battery.

## Do: turn it round (4 minutes, optional)

Connect a small toy motor to a multimeter set to its lowest DC volts range. Wind a string round the motor's shaft and pull it quickly, or spin the shaft hard between your fingers. What does the meter show? Read the first part of **Electromagnetic induction** ("The rule") to find out why a motor can also be a **generator**.

## Explain it to the guide (8 minutes)

Your guide will ask each of you about your motor. Be ready to:

- show it spinning by itself,
- explain why a current near a magnet feels a push, and what happens when you reverse it,
- explain what the half-scraped leg does, and what would happen if both legs were fully scraped,
- say which change made your motor fastest, and why,
- name four motors in daily life. Think of a ceiling fan, a bajaj's starter motor, a water pump, and the tiny motor that makes your phone vibrate.
