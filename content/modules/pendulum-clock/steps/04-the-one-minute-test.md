## Do: the one-minute test (10 minutes)

Set your tuned pendulum swinging with a small pull. One person watches the phone stopwatch, one counts **ticks** (every time the nut passes the middle), and one watches for mistakes.

Start the stopwatch on a tick and say "zero". Stop it on tick **60** (that is 30 full swings). A perfect seconds pendulum takes **60.0 s**.

Do it twice. If you're more than 1 s out, adjust the length by a few millimetres and try again. Write down both times.

## Back to the mystery (5 minutes)

Open your page titled *The beat*. Next to each prediction, write ✓ or ✗ and what you found. How close was your guess for the length?

Answer the guide's question in your own words: **what decides how fast a pendulum swings?** Use these words: *length, mass, small swings, period, fair test*.

## Predict: bigger and smaller clocks (5 minutes)

Use your T² graph (extend the line if you need to) or the pattern "four times the length, twice the period".

1. A pendulum that ticks every **2 seconds** has a period of 4 s. How long must it be?
2. A pendulum that does one **full swing** every second (period 1 s). How long?
3. An old wall clock is running **slow**: it loses time. Should you make its pendulum longer or shorter?

## Explain it to the guide (5 minutes)

Be ready to:

- show your fair-test table and say which things changed the period and which didn't,
- explain why you timed 10 swings, not one,
- show your T² graph and how you used it to predict the length,
- run your pendulum against the stopwatch,
- answer the three questions above.
