## Do: set up your pendulum (5 minutes)

1. Tie one nut firmly to the end of the string.
2. Push the top of the string into a binder clip and clip it onto the stick. The clip's jaws are the point the pendulum swings from.
3. Slide the string through the clip until the length is **50 cm**. Measure from the bottom of the clip's jaws to the **middle of the nut**.

## Theory: frequency and period

Read the sections *They are opposites* and *Measuring them in the lab* in **Frequency and period**. The key idea: one swing is too quick to time well. Your thumb on the stopwatch is late by about 0.2 s every time. So you time **10 full swings** and divide by 10. The 0.2 s mistake is shared over 10 swings, so it becomes only 0.02 s per swing.

## How to time 10 swings

1. Pull the nut a little to one side (about 10 cm) and let go. Don't push it.
2. Let it swing once or twice to settle.
3. When the nut reaches the far left, start the stopwatch and say **"zero"**.
4. Count "one" each time it comes back to the far left. On **"ten"**, stop.
5. Period = time ÷ 10.

Practise once. Counting "one" at the start, instead of "zero", is the most common mistake: it gives only 9 swings.

## Theory: fair tests

Read the section *Three kinds of variable* in **Fair tests and averages**. In each test below, you change **one** thing and keep everything else the same.

## Do: three fair tests (20 minutes)

Time every setting **twice**. If the two times differ by more than 0.4 s, do a third.

| Test | Change this | Keep the same |
|---|---|---|
| A: mass | 1 nut, then 3 nuts taped in a tight bunch | length 50 cm (to the middle of the bunch), 10 cm pull |
| B: size of swing | pull back 5 cm, then 20 cm | length 50 cm, 1 nut |
| C: length | 25 cm, then 100 cm | 1 nut, small pull (about 10 cm) |

For test A, check the length again after taping on the extra nuts: the middle of the bunch must still be 50 cm below the clip.

## Notice something puzzling

A heavier nut is pulled down harder by gravity. So why doesn't it swing faster? And why does a nut pulled back 20 cm, which has further to travel, take the same time as one pulled back 5 cm?

(Here's a hint: the heavy nut is pulled harder, but it is also harder to get moving. The two effects cancel exactly. And the nut pulled further moves faster, which almost exactly makes up for the longer path, as long as the swings stay small.)

## Record

Make a table: test, setting, time 1, time 2, mean time, period. Then write one sentence for each test: "Changing the ___ does / does not change the period." Compare with your predictions on *The beat*.
