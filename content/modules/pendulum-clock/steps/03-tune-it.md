## Do: five lengths (15 minutes)

Only the length matters, so now find out **how** it matters. Use one nut and small swings (about 10 cm).

Time 10 full swings at each of these lengths, twice each: **25, 40, 60, 80 and 100 cm**. Always measure from the clip's jaws to the middle of the nut.

| Length L (m) | Time for 10 swings (s) | Mean (s) | Period T (s) | T² (s²) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 | | | | |
| 0.40 | | | | |

To get T², multiply the period by itself: a period of 1.5 s gives T² = 1.5 × 1.5 = 2.25.

## Theory: graphs

Read the sections *Drawing a good graph* and *Reading the shape* in **Graphs and relationships**. Length is what you chose, so it goes on the x-axis.

## Do: two graphs (10 minutes)

1. Plot **T** (y-axis) against **L** (x-axis). Draw a smooth line of best fit. Is it straight?
2. Now plot **T²** (y-axis) against **L** (x-axis), on a new set of axes that both start at zero. Draw a straight line of best fit.

## Notice something puzzling

The first graph curves: the period grows, but more and more slowly. Look at your table. Going from 25 cm to 100 cm makes the string **four** times longer, but the period only **doubles**.

You have met this pattern before. Read *The square-root pattern* in **Free fall and square roots**: four times the distance, twice the time. The pendulum follows the same kind of rule. That's why T² against L comes out straight: squaring undoes the square root.

## Do: predict, then tune (15 minutes)

A pendulum that ticks once a second has a period of **2 s**, so T² = **4 s²**.

1. On your straight-line graph, find 4 on the T² axis. Go across to your line, then down to the L axis. That is your predicted length. Write it down.
2. Set your pendulum to that length. Time 10 full swings. You want **20.0 s**.
3. Too slow (more than 20 s)? Shorten the string a little. Too fast? Lengthen it. Change it by 1 cm at a time.
4. When two timings in a row are between 19.8 s and 20.2 s, mark the string at the clip with a marker. Your pendulum is tuned.

## Record

Write down your predicted length and your final tuned length. How close was the prediction? Work out the **gradient** of your T² line (rise ÷ run). Every team should get a number close to the same value. Compare with another team.
