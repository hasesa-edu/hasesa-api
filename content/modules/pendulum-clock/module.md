For about 300 years, the best clocks in the world were kept on time by a weight swinging on a rod. In two hours you find out what controls a pendulum's beat, and build one that ticks once every second.

**Words we use.** One **full swing** is there and back again. The time it takes is the **period**. A **tick** is one swing from one side to the other, so there are two ticks in every full swing. A pendulum that ticks once a second has a period of 2 seconds. That needs a length of about 1 metre.

1. **The ticking padlock** (15 min). Watch the guide's pendulum keep time with a phone, and predict what controls its beat.
2. **What sets the beat?** (35 min). Fair tests: does the mass, the size of the swing, or the length change the period?
3. **Tune it** (45 min). Time five lengths, draw a graph, use it to predict the right length, and tune your pendulum.
4. **The one-minute test** (25 min). Check your pendulum against a phone stopwatch, solve the mystery, and predict a clock that ticks every 2 seconds.

By the end you'll know how to measure a period accurately, how to run a fair test, and how to turn a curved graph into a straight one that makes predictions easy.
