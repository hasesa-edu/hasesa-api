## The hot-water rules first

Copy these into the front of your notebook:

1. Only the guide pours hot water, from a thermos flask. Never touch a kettle.
2. Cups stay on the tray. Bring the wrapping to the cup, not the cup to the wrapping.
3. Wipe up spills at once.
4. If hot water touches your skin, tell the guide and hold it under cool running water for 20 minutes.

## Watch

Before you arrived, your guide filled a thermos and an open cup with the same hot water, at the same time. Now the guide pours a little from each into two clean cups and measures the temperatures.

Then hold your hand **near** (not touching) each one. Touch the outside of the thermos. Touch the outside of a cup that has just been filled.

## Notice something puzzling

The thermos has no fire inside it, no electricity, nothing that makes heat. It can't make the water hotter. All it can do is **stop heat from leaving**. But heat leaves a cup of shai or buna so easily. Where does it go, and how does it get out?

## Predict

On a page titled *The thermos mystery*, write:

1. **How does heat escape from an open cup?** List every way you can think of.
2. **What is special about a thermos?** Write your best guess.
3. **Guess:** a cup of water at 60 °C sits on the table for 30 minutes. What will its temperature be? Each person writes a number. You'll measure it in step 3.

## Questions

As a team, list your questions. For example:

- Why does steam come off a hot cup?
- Does a lid make a big difference or a small one?
- Is a shiny thermos better than a painted one?

Today you'll try to build something nearly as good as a thermos, from newspaper, cloth and foil.
